We refuse

The manifesto, in full

We refuse the silent kitchen.

The pass that goes quiet, because there's nobody to call it anymore. The algorithm making a stock instead of a singing commis. "Efficiency" whispered instead of "BEHIND" yelled — and nobody notices, until silent becomes soulless.

That future is coming for kitchens the way it came for everything else — quietly, reasonably, one small convenience at a time.

We're not interested in being reasonable.

We believe the chef is the whole point. Not the obstacle to automate around. Not the variable to optimise out. The point.

Every system we build exists to keep the kitchen loud, to put more time, more trust, more craft back into their hands, never to take it from them. So we do the unglamorous thing. We build the full stack ourselves, because we won't hand the leverage over kitchens to people who've never worked a shift. We speak in the language of people, not products. We treat every chef we meet as already capable of excellence, not someone we're here to fix, but someone we're here to serve.

We hold ourselves to the standard of the rooms we most admire. You've been earning your craft for years already, before we've earned the right to matter. So we run like we're cooking for a Michelin table every single day, even when no one's watching, especially when no one's watching. We're protective of what we've built and outrageously generous within it. We prove ourselves where it's hardest first, because trust built in an easy room isn't trust at all.

We are not here to replace the hand that plates the food. We are here to stand behind it, so the kitchen never has to go quiet to survive.

The kitchen stays loud. The craft stays human. The chef stays the hero of the story.

This is what we refuse to lose.

What SINTH measures

The craft is the subject. The person never is.
Measured
  • Hands, technique, timing
  • Movement between station and pass
  • Ingredient handling, prep sequence
  • Where time is actually spent in a service
Never measured
  • Faces
  • Raw audio
  • Identity
  • Anything that isn't the craft itself
Why it's built this way
This isn't a compliance decision dressed up as a value. It's a legal protection, a social licence strategy and a product position at once — the same design choice serving all three, in every venue and every regulatory conversation, from day one.

Photography

Hands and equipment, never faces
The register
Black and white, motion blur used deliberately — the visual register of a fast, loud kitchen, and the thing that keeps hands legible while a face stays soft. No posed, fully-identifiable "team at work" photography. This performs the same test as the data itself: does it measure the craft, or the person?
Sourcing
Bespoke, commissioned SINTH photography is still an open item. These hold the register until it exists.

SINTH is the data behind the chef's hands.
